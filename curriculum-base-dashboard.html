Curriculum Architecture

The shared baseline creates common expectations for responsible AI use; the pathways translate those expectations into academic, teaching, operational, and strategic practice.

Rutgers AI Essentials Baseline

1

AI foundations and limitations

Common language for capabilities, limits, hallucinations, bias, and human accountability.

2

Approved Rutgers tools

Use protected Rutgers accounts for university work and avoid unsupported personal tools.

3

Prompting and review

Combine sources, audience, timeframe, and output format, then verify the result.

4

Data privacy and classification

Check FERPA, PHI, research data, proprietary data, library records, and critical data before use.

5

Academic integrity and disclosure

Course and instructor rules govern academic use; students should not assume AI is allowed.

6

Copyright and fair use

Review source rights, attribution, licensing limits, and AI-generated content risks.

7

Accessibility and inclusion

Design AI use around equitable access, accessibility review, and bias awareness.

    Example Examples

    How Rutgers Should Integrate Google Tools Into The Foundational Curriculum

    1

    Approve The Use Case

    Start with Rutgers-approved accounts, course policy, accessibility expectations, and data classification before students use a tool.

    2

    Ground In Sources

    Use library databases, instructor readings, and licensed materials only when terms allow. Require source lists and evidence trails.

    3

    Practice Learning

    Use Gemini, NotebookLM, and practice tests for explanation, review, retrieval practice, and self-assessment, not answer substitution.

    4

    Disclose And Verify

    Students document prompts, sources, AI help received, verification steps, and what they changed after review.

    5

    Assess Judgment

    Faculty and librarians assess source quality, citation accuracy, fair use reasoning, reflection, and learning outcomes.

    Library Component

    • Teach students how to build NotebookLM source sets from appropriate scholarly and course materials.
    • Flag licensed-content limits before uploading readings, datasets, media, or database exports into AI tools.
    • Provide checklists for fabricated citations, weak evidence, missing viewpoints, and unsupported summaries.
    • Co-design assignments that require AI workflow notes, source verification, and fair use reflection.

    Foundational Examples

    • First-year writing: compare a Gemini outline with library-source evidence and revise with citations.
    • Biology: use NotebookLM to study instructor-approved articles, then verify claims against primary literature.
    • History: build a source-grounded notebook and identify what the AI summary omits from primary sources.
    • Data science: use Gemini to explain code concepts, then test results independently and document errors.

    Libraries, Copyright, And Fair Use

    University Libraries should be curriculum partners across all pathways, especially for source evaluation, licensing, scholarly communication, and library-record privacy.

    Library Co-Owned Modules

    AI literacy Source evaluation Citation practice Copyright Fair use Licensing limits Scholarly communication Research data Library-record privacy

    Minimum Standard

    • Teach that AI output may contain unattributed copyrighted material.
    • Teach fair use as contextual, not automatic.
    • Check license terms before AI ingestion or text/data mining.
    • Require Libraries approval for library circulation or use records.

    Risk Controls

    These controls translate Rutgers AI guidance into decisions learners can make before using AI for university work.

    Approved Tool Check

    Use Rutgers-approved versions for university work. External accounts should not receive university data unless formally approved.

    Data Classification Check

    Screen for confidential, critical, FERPA, PHI, IRB, research, proprietary, library-record, financial, and infrastructure data.

    Academic Integrity Check

    Faculty state course rules clearly. Students follow course-specific permission and disclosure expectations.

    Copyright Check

    Review attribution, fair use, license terms, market effect, and whether AI generated or transformed protected material.

    Output Review Check

    Verify accuracy, bias, missing citations, invented sources, accessibility, and audience appropriateness before use.

    Purchase Review Check

    New AI technology should pass security, accessibility, privacy, procurement, and vendor-risk review.

    Implementation Roadmap

    A five-phase rollout gives Rutgers a standard model while leaving room for campus and discipline adaptation.

    1. Governance

    Name sponsors, OIT owner, Libraries co-owner, academic affairs owner, and campus implementation leads.

    2. Baseline Launch

    Publish AI Essentials, approved tool guidance, data classification aid, and syllabus/disclosure templates.

    3. Pathways

    Deploy student badges, the Teaching GenAI Pathway, faculty community of practice, staff labs, and leadership workshops.

    4. Advanced Tracks

    Add research, health sciences, libraries, law, business, STEM, humanities, creative practice, and NJ AI Hub Faculty Futures-aligned variants.

    5. Improvement

    Review each semester using analytics, surveys, help desk trends, integrity trends, showcase artifacts, and NJ AI Hub resource updates.
